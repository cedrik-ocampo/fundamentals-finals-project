# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Anime fans who want to log, rate, and review every anime they watch. They use the app in their own time — after finishing an episode or series — to record impressions, build curated lists, and follow friends with similar taste. They're invested hobbyists, not casual viewers; the app is part of how they take their fandom seriously.

## Product Purpose

AniTrack is a Letterboxd-style anime tracker: log every episode with half-star ratings and watch dates, write reviews, build lists, and follow the activity of friends. Success means the app feels like a personal archive of someone's anime life — accurate, expressive, and worth returning to.

## Brand Personality

Passionate · Cinematic · Obsessive. The app should feel like it was built by and for fans who care deeply — not a generic startup product or a corporate database. Every screen carries the intensity of someone who has rewatched their favourite series multiple times.

## Anti-references

- **MyAnimeList (MAL)**: cluttered list-database feel, zero visual personality, overwhelming tables and metadata — avoid the legacy anime tracker aesthetic.
- **Generic SaaS / startup**: cream backgrounds, hero-metric dashboards, rounded ghost-card grids, AI-scaffold layouts — avoid the 2025 startup product look.
- **Busy anime fan sites**: over-designed, too many colours, flashy banners, chaotic otaku-site aesthetic — avoid visual noise that competes with the content.

## Design Principles

1. **Content first, chrome second.** Anime cover art, titles, and personal ratings are the product. The UI frames them without competing.
2. **Fan-made, not corporate.** Prefer sharp, intentional choices over safe defaults. The dark palette and violet accent are commitments, not templates.
3. **Earn every interaction.** Actions (log, review, follow) should feel responsive and satisfying — micro-feedback matters at every touch point.
4. **Obsession has a rhythm.** Diary entries, stats, and lists should feel like a personal record — organised but lived-in, not sterile.
5. **Dark is the default, contrast is non-negotiable.** The dark-anime aesthetic is identity, not fashion; WCAG AA on every text element, always.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: 4.5:1 contrast minimum for all body text, 3:1 for large text and UI components. Full keyboard navigation. Screen reader support via semantic HTML and aria-labels on icon-only controls. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` on all transitions and animations.
